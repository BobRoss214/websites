# The account demo (pretend accounts)

**This is a demo for Mrs. Cathy and Rob. It is pretend.** It shows what customer accounts could do for the farm: a family signs in, sees its visits, asks to change a day or a pizza order, and gets an answer from Mrs. Cathy or the kitchen. Everything is kept in one browser on one computer. Nothing goes to a server and nothing is sent to anyone. There are no real people, no real phone numbers and no real addresses in it, except the farm's own address that is already on the site.

It is hidden from real visitors. Only the person who shows it sees it.

## How to show it

1. Open the home page with `?demo` at the end of the address, for example `index.html?demo` on your computer or `https://www.wiseacresorganic.com/?demo` once the site is online. The claude.ai preview of the site has the demo switched on by itself.
2. A round person button appears next to the language button in the top bar. It opens the family's account page (`account.html`).
3. A yellow strip under the top bar says "Demo: pretend accounts. Nothing is saved on a server or sent to anyone." It stays on every demo screen. It holds three links, **Family**, **Mrs. Cathy's desk** and **Kitchen**, a **Start over** button and, when the tour is on, **Take a tour**.
4. On the account page press **Create account** (or **Fill in sample details** and then **Create account**). The form asks one plain question: "Do you plan or manage a party, an event or a school trip?" It has four big buttons: **No**, **Yes: a party**, **Yes: a school trip** and **Yes: another event**. **No** is chosen to begin with, and **Fill in sample details** fills in the name and the email and chooses the school trip. The page loads a sample family: Alex, Jordan, Riley (8), Casey (6) and baby Sam (2), one visit next Saturday with pizza and two past visits. Only a yes adds an event of that kind. A green line says "We added a sample visit so you can try the buttons."
5. The password is pretend: any password works and it is never saved.
6. Try a change on the family side, for example **Request a different day**. The visit says "Waiting for a reply from Mrs. Cathy". Then open **Mrs. Cathy's desk** (it is the same browser, so it sees the request), press Yes, No or Suggest, and go back to **Family** to see the answer. Pizza changes go to the kitchen the same way.
7. The bell on the account page shows the newest notices (answers, alerts, weather) with a count. Each one leads to the right tab.
8. The **My event** tab is on the bar only for a family that has an event: one that said yes at sign-up, or one that added an event later. Without an event the tab is not there, and the address `account.html#event` opens the first tab. A family that said No can add one with the small link **I also plan an event** at the bottom of **My family**: it asks for the kind (a party, a school trip or another event), adds a sample one and opens the tab.

The three pages remember that the demo is on while you move between them in the same browser tab. A new tab needs `?demo` again.

## The desk in a pretend rush

Mrs. Cathy and Rob's desk (`farm-desk.html`) can show a busy day. The box **Right now** at the top holds three things.

- **The strip "Pretend rush"**, the same control as on the kitchen screen. **On** starts the rush. **Off** pauses it: nothing comes and the pretend clock stands still. **Calm**, **Busy** and **Rush** set how fast things come (about one every 40, 18 or 8 seconds, some of them for the kitchen). It is off until somebody presses On. The first start also makes four things that are already waiting, so the screen is not empty. The choice is kept in the demo, so every screen agrees. **Start over** clears it.
- **Four counters**: **Waiting for you** (requests and questions), **Photos and reviews to look at**, **Answered today** and **People waiting for news**. The first, second and fourth link to their part of the screen. They are read from the demo's own records, so a reload or a second window says the same (**Answered today** also uses the rush's count of the answers given on the desk, so it never shrinks when the rush tidies old pretend things away).
- **Just now**: the last six things that happened, newest first, in plain words ("Dana's family asks: Move to Sunday, Nov 29 at 12:00 pm", "You said Yes to Dana's family."), each with how long ago ("just now", "3 min ago"). While the rush is Off, the clocks stand still, and so do these words.

On a window 900 pixels wide or more the counters (two by two) and **Just now** sit side by side, so the first screen of a laptop (1366 x 768) or a tablet (1024 x 768) shows the strip, the four counters and the newest three lines. Narrower windows keep one column. The box says **Pretend time: 11:00 am** once the rush has made things (the rush's clock follows the real farm clock between 10 am and 3 pm and starts at 11:00 am outside those hours); before that it says **Time:** and the real one.

The rush sends the desk a family that asks to move a visit, a question, a photo or a review. Every pretend family has a name of its own ("Dana's family") on its card, in the feed and in "This week". The family's own screens never see them, and answering one never puts a notice in the family's bell.

On a busy desk:

- New cards go to the bottom of **Waiting for you**, oldest first, with a **New** mark for half a minute. The cards you are reading do not move, and a note you are typing keeps its words and its cursor.
- When new cards arrive out of sight, a small button says how many (**Show 3 new cards**) and takes you to the first one. It floats at the bottom of the screen and waits while the **Right now** box is there (the counters and the feed already show what came in, and the button would lie over them); it comes as soon as the box has been scrolled away. A screen reader hears the arrivals politely, in batches (one message every four seconds at most). While the rush is on, the title of the browser tab counts what waits, for example `(4) Demo: Mrs. Cathy's desk`. The desk makes no sound.
- **Yes**, **No**, **Suggest another day or time**, **Send reply**, **Approve** and **Not now** work on a pretend family as on a real one. The card leaves the waiting list at once and shows under **Answered** (the latest six). The keyboard focus goes to the next card. A pretend family answers a suggestion by itself a few seconds later, and the card says OK or no thanks.
- **Nobody answers, the rush goes on.** At most 10 things wait for the desk (8 for the kitchen). When a side is full and a new thing is due, the family of the oldest thing gives up, if it has waited at least 2 minutes (the kitchen's things: after their answer-by time and 30 seconds more, so they were late, and beeped if Sound was on, before they are missed). A change of day is taken back (it shows under **Answered** as "The family did not wait and took this request back." and in **Just now**); a question, a photo or a review simply leaves **Waiting for you**. The strip says how many gave up ("On: pretend families are sending things (60 so far, 40 gave up waiting)."). If both sides are full and nothing has waited long enough yet (a few seconds at the most, at Rush), the strip says "On, but full: new things come when some are answered or time out". A card that goes while you are writing to it says so and counts nothing. Rush makes about 7 things a minute this way, Busy about 3, Calm about 1.5.
- To keep the screen short: at most 24 waiting cards are drawn (a line says how many more wait), "This week" shows five visits a day and says how many more, and the rush keeps only its newest forty things.

## The pizza kitchen

The kitchen screen (`kitchen.html`) is made for a busy pizza kitchen: look at a ticket, press one key, and the next ticket is ready. It is built for a tablet on a counter (big type, easy to read from 2 metres), and it works with a finger, a mouse or only the keyboard. Nothing is real: the tickets are the sample family's pizza order and its changes, and the pretend families of the rush.

- **Tickets.** Every pizza order and every change a family asks for is a ticket. It shows a big order number (the sample family's is 101, the pretend families' are 200 and more), the family, the pizzas, the pick-up time, how long it waited and the family's note. A change also shows its words and a small table of the old time and the asked time. The tickets that need an answer stand in one list, **Needs an answer**: late ones on top, then the soonest deadline. Beside it (under it on a phone) **With the family** holds the plans the kitchen sent and the family has not answered, and **Done** the last six answered tickets. The first screen is made for a cook who only looks: when the screen opens with tickets, again after the page has loaded (a reload does not put the page back at its top), when the first tickets arrive and when the rush is switched on, the page is scrolled until the FIRST ticket, with its pizzas and its three buttons, sits just under what sticks to the top (the site's header and the kitchen's own strip). This is done only while the page is at its top or where the screen put it, and never while the tour is open. The demo bar stays at the top of the page on this screen (it does not follow the window), so the board gets its height. The strip with **Waiting**, **Answered**, **Late**, the clock, **Sound** and **Keys** sticks under the header (not on a phone), a ticket that gets the focus from the keyboard goes to the top of the board area, the small editor says which order it is for, and the tab title shows the count ("(3 late) ..." or "(5) ..."). The type and the column grow with the window: one unit (`--u`, 20 px up to 1279 px wide, 22 px at 1366, 27 px from 1600, 32 px at 1920, 38 px at 2560) sets every size of a ticket, and the page's column grows from 1180 px to 1240, 1500, 1700 and 2100 px. A change ticket's table uses the same big sizes as an order (at 1366: pizza names 32 px, values 30 px, the timer 38 px). The lanes sit beside the tickets from 1500 px, below the tickets under that.
- **One press answers.** **Yes** is the key 1 (or Y), **No** is 2 (or N) and **Another time** is 3 (or S). A new order has no No: it is confirmed with Yes, or moved with Another time. Up and Down move between tickets. A key held down answers one ticket only (an auto-repeat is ignored). After an answer the next ticket that needs one gets the focus ("keep rolling"). The number and letter keys work from anywhere on the page, so the button **Keys** (beside Sound in the strip at the top, On to begin with) turns them all off, the key marks on the buttons and the line of keys with them; the buttons keep working. The choice lasts until the page is closed. For the sample family an answer goes through the store as it always did: Yes puts the change into the order and tells the family. Nothing is ever told to a pretend family's bell.
- **Another time.** Opens a small editor on the ticket. The time starts from what the family asked for. The buttons **+5**, **+10**, **+15** and **-5** change it, so do the plus and minus keys, Up and Down (5 minutes) and Page Up and Page Down (15). A note for the family is optional. **Enter** sends, **Esc** closes the editor and sends nothing. Send does nothing until the time or the note has changed.
- **The timer.** Every ticket has an answer-by timer: 3:00 for a new order, 2:00 for a change. The strip **Answer by** under the board changes them in steps of 30 seconds (30 seconds to 10 minutes). The bar is green while more than half is left, amber until 15 percent is left, red below that, and the words say it too ("1:40 left"). At zero the ticket is **Late**: it turns red, goes to the top of the list and stays there. The sample family's ticket starts its timer when the kitchen first sees it. The timers run only while the pretend rush is On: Off pauses even the clock (the screen says paused).
- **Sound.** Off until someone presses **Sound** (a browser does not allow sound before a press). Then a soft chime for tickets that arrive and a gentle beep when a ticket turns late and every 7 seconds while any is late, never while the rush is paused and never in a tab nobody can see (the late ticket is still late when the tab comes back, and beeps again then). **Sound** again, or the M key, mutes. People who asked their device for less motion get no pulse on a late ticket.
- **Counters** at the top: Waiting, Answered (the demo keeps the count, so it never shrinks when old pretend tickets are tidied away) and Late, with a small pretend clock. A polite status line (`aria-live`) tells a screen reader when a ticket arrives, when one is late and what was answered. The timer is a timer that does not read itself out; each ticket also says "Answer by 12:35 pm".
- **The pretend rush** works as on the desk: the strip **Pretend rush** (On, Off, Calm, Busy, Rush) is at the top of the kitchen screen too, and it is the same switch (the choice is kept in the demo, so every screen agrees). It is off until somebody presses On, so the kitchen first shows only the sample family's tickets. The first start also makes four things that are already waiting. About half of what the rush sends goes to the kitchen: new orders (pizzas from the site's own menu, pick-up times inside the farm's day) and changes to orders the kitchen already confirmed (a later or earlier time, one more or fewer pizza, a topping left off, a gluten-free crust, a note). When the kitchen suggests another time, the pretend family answers by itself 5 to 18 seconds later, OK in about six cases in seven.

## How to start over

Press **Start over** in the yellow strip, then **Yes, start over**. It clears the pretend account, visits, orders and requests in this browser. Nothing else on the site is touched. Closing the browser does not clear the demo: the next time you open it with `?demo`, the same family is still there.

## How to turn it on and off

It is **off** unless one of these is true:

- the address has `?demo` (the page then remembers it for that browser tab), or
- the page sets `window.WA_DEMO = true` before the scripts run. The claude.ai preview of the site does this.

Being on your own computer (`localhost`) does **not** turn it on, so the normal checks never see the demo. With the demo off:

- there is no round person button anywhere,
- the three demo pages show only a polite box ("This is a demo page. It is not open to visitors.") with a link to the home page,
- the pages are marked `noindex`, are not in `sitemap.xml`, are not in the menu, and the host is told not to list them (`_headers`),
- the demo scripts keep nothing in the browser: the one switch is `window.WA_DEMO_MODE()` in `js/demo-header.js`, and it reads no browser storage.

To remove the demo from the site completely, delete the three page files in `pages/` (`account.html`, `farm-desk.html`, `kitchen.html`), the `js/demo-*.js` and `css/demo*.css` files, the `js/demo-header.js` line in `index.html`, and run the three rebuild commands below.

## What a real version would need

The demo does not answer these questions. They are for a later talk:

- A safe place for logins. The demo has none: it has no server.
- A link to the booking tool (Bookeo) so a visit change really changes the booking. The booking tool may already do part of this. It is worth asking.
- Alerts to Mrs. Cathy and the kitchen when a request arrives (email or text). In the demo they only see it when someone opens their screen.
- Care with children's information: names, ages and allergies are personal. A real version needs a rule for who sees them and how long they are kept.
- An updated privacy page (`docs/WHAT_THE_SITE_STORES.md` says today that the site keeps almost nothing).
- Someone to answer the requests every day.

### Adding a screen to the account page

One call: `DemoApp.view('pizza', { label: 'My pizza', render: function (root, ctx) { ... } })`.

- `root` is an empty element inside the tab's panel. The shell has already printed the tab's heading (an `h2` with the label).
- `ctx` has `store`, `data`, `ui`, `app`, `account`, `today`, `params`, `go(name, params)` and `rerender()`.
- The shell clears `root` and calls `render` again after every change of the store, but not while a field of the view has the focus. A screen never subscribes itself and never writes to the store while it draws.
- The tabs are `#visits` (the first one), `#pizza`, `#family`, `#alerts`, `#messages`, `#photos` and `#event`. A tab shows only when its file registered it. A screen can also give `shownIf: function (store) { return true or false }` to say when its tab is on the bar: My event shows only when the family has an event. A `#hash` that is none of these, or the hash of a tab that is not shown, shows the first tab and is left alone. `go('photos', { visit: 'v2' })` opens a tab and hands over `params` in memory, so the address stays a plain `#photos`.
- Do not give an element the id of a tab (`pizza`, `event` ...): the browser would scroll to it.
- Use `DemoUI.toast(text)` for a short message and `DemoUI.modal(...)` or `DemoUI.confirm(...)` for a box. There is one toast place and one modal for the whole demo. `DemoUI.closeModals()` closes every dialog that is open (the top one first, each by its own close, so the focus goes back and a `confirm` answers no) and says how many there were; the tour uses it when the owner leaves a card with a dialog still open. `DemoUI.clearToasts()` takes away every toast that is on the screen now (a dialog stays, the store is not touched) and says how many there were; the tour uses it when the owner leaves any card, so an old message (the kitchen's "suggests something else") does not sit on top of the next card. `DemoUI.choices({ name, legend, options, value })` draws a question with big buttons (real radio buttons; the sign-up question is one). Every field is built in JavaScript: no page file holds an `<input>`, a `<textarea>` or a `<select>`.

### The store (`js/demo-store.js`)

All methods are synchronous and return plain copies. The state lives in the browser's storage under the one key `wa-demo-v1`. If the storage is blocked or full the store carries on in memory (`saveStatus()` says `ok` or `memory`). Other tabs update live. `DemoStore.create(storage, { now })` makes a store on any storage with `getItem`, `setItem` and `removeItem` and a clock you give it: the tests use that.

| Area | Names |
|---|---|
| Account | `createAccount({ name, email, eventKind })` (no argument: the sample account Alex), `signIn(email)`, `signOut()`, `current()`, `hasAccount(email)`, `reset()`, `seedSample()`, `addEvent(kind)` |
| Lists | `list(coll)`, `get(coll, id)`, `add(coll, obj)`, `update(coll, id, patch)`, `remove(coll, id)`. Lists: `visits`, `orders`, `requests`, `messages`, `notices`, `reviews`, `photos`, `events`, `waitlist`. `list(coll)` is the signed-in family's own records; `list(coll, { live: true })` adds the pretend families' (see "The pretend rush"); `openRequests(to, { live: true })` and `threadsWaiting({ live: true })` too. |
| Single objects | `get('family')`, `update('family', patch)` or `update('family', null, patch)`; the same for `alerts`; `getFamily()`, `setFamily(patch)`; `list('closedDays')`; `flag(key)`, `setFlag(key, bool)` |
| Requests | `ask({ kind, to, target: { coll, id }, patch, from, summary, note, weather })`, `reply(id, { answer: 'yes'/'no'/'suggest', note, suggestion: { patch, summary } })`, `respond(id, 'ok'/'decline')`, `cancel(id)`, `openRequestFor(coll, id)`, `requestsFor(coll, id)`, `openRequests(to)` |
| Messages and notices | `post(thread, 'family'/'cathy', text)`, `thread(id)`, `threadsWaiting()`, `postNotice(text, link, to)`, `noticesFor(to)`, `markRead(id)`, `markAllRead(to)` |
| Weather | `closeDay(date, reason)`, `openDay(date)`, `isClosed(date)`, `openDays(from, package, n)` |
| Tell me when | `setAlert(key, bool)`, `alertCounts()`, `fireAlert(key, day)`; where to send the messages: `getNotify()`, `setNotify({ text, email })` |
| Reviews and photos | `submitReview({ visitId, stars, text, photoName, thumb })`, `decideReview(id, bool)`, `submitPhoto({ visitId, name, thumb })`, `decidePhoto(id, bool)` |
| Other | `subscribe(fn)` (returns the function that stops it), `now()`, `today()`, `snapshot()`, `saveStatus()`, `transact(fn)` (runs `fn(state)` on a private copy of the whole state and saves what it returns; only the rush uses it), `get('live')` (the rush's own bookkeeping) |

**The sign-up question and My event.** `createAccount({ name, email, eventKind })` takes the answer to "Do you plan or manage a party, an event or a school trip?": `'none'` (the button **No**), `'party'`, `'school'` or `'other'`. The account keeps it as `eventKind` (read it with `current().eventKind`), and the sample it loads has an event of that kind: with `'none'` it has none. Leaving `eventKind` out gives the ready-made sample, which has the school trip (so does `createAccount()` with no argument, and `DemoData.SAMPLE_ACCOUNT` says `eventKind: 'school'`); anything that is not one of the four is read as `'none'`. `seedSample()` always loads the ready-made sample, with the school trip, and the account keeps `'school'`: the tour starts from it. `addEvent(kind)` is "I also plan an event": it adds the sample event of that kind (`'party'`, `'school'` or `'other'`) with Mrs. Cathy's first message in its thread, and the account keeps the kind; it does nothing (the answer is `null`) when the family already has an event or the kind is not one of those three. The My event tab is shown exactly when `list('events')` is not empty. The sign-up card is laid out so that a computer shows the whole question on its first screen (a 1440 x 900 window): the name and the email sit side by side (`.demo-pair` in `css/demo.css`, two columns when each gets 14rem or more, one under the other on a phone) and the question comes right after the email, before the pretend password (which nothing reads).

**One pattern for every change: a request.** Ask, waiting, reply, the family says OK, done. Nothing changes until the farm says yes. The statuses are `waiting`, `yes`, `no`, `suggested`, `accepted`, `declined` and `cancelled`. A visit and an order have at most one open request (`waiting` or `suggested`): `ask` gives `null` when there is one. A reply of yes applies the `patch` to the target. A suggestion keeps its own patch until the family answers OK (it is applied) or No thanks (nothing changes). Every step is written into the request's `history` with a time and a plain sentence, and makes a notice for the other side.

Two rules are built in. When a visit moves to another time, the pizza pick-up moves with it (1 hour after the visit) unless the family chose its own pick-up time. When a visit moves, its `closed` mark is worked out again for the new day.

### The shapes

- **Request kinds:** `visit-change` (goes to Mrs. Cathy; a weather move has `weather: true`), `pizza-change` (goes to the kitchen), `event-change` (goes to Mrs. Cathy).
- **visit:** `{ id, date, time, package: 'pizza' or 'farm', party: { adults, kids: [{ name, age }] }, addons: [{ id: 'wagon' / 'barrel' / 'corn', qty }], paid: { receipt, on, lines: [{ id, label, qty, unit, amount }], total }, closed, closedReason }`. There is no status: a visit is past when its date is before today.
- **order:** `{ id, visitId, pickup: 'HH:MM', pizzas: [{ type, count, toppings: [...], glutenFree, vegan }], notes, status: 'new' or 'confirmed' }`. `type` is an id from `DemoData.PIZZAS`.
- **request:** `{ id, kind, to, from, target, patch, before, summary, note, weather, status, suggestion, reply, history: [{ at, who, text }], created, updated }`. `before` holds the target's values for the patch keys when the request was made, so a screen can show before and after.
- **family:** `{ name, adults: [{ name }], kids: [{ name, age }], allergies, glutenFree, vegan, phone, notifyBy }`. `phone` and `notifyBy` belong to Tell me when (see "The Tell me when screen" below).
- **account:** `{ id, name, email, created, eventKind }` with `eventKind` one of `'none'`, `'party'`, `'school'`, `'other'`.
- **event:** one per family. A school trip is `{ id, kind: 'school', title, date, students, guests, slipsReceived, thread }`: $11 for each student, and $11 for each parent or sibling age 3 or older who comes; the minimum is 100 students. A party is `{ id, kind: 'party', title, date, guests, thread }`: one price for the whole party ($300, the farm entry fee included), up to 50 guests; children age 2 and younger are free and do not count. Another event is `{ id, kind: 'other', title, date, guests, thread }`: no price, because the site names none. `DemoData.priceEvent(event)` gives `{ lines, total }` (`total` is `null` for another event) and `DemoData.eventProblem(event)` the page's rule in plain words (or an empty text). The numbers of a sample (the head counts, the permission slips, the dates) are made up; no start time or deposit is shown, because the site gives none. A head-count request changes `{ students, guests }` for a school trip and `{ guests }` for the others.
- **contacts:** `DemoData.CONTACTS` holds the two e-mail addresses the pretend account shows, both already on the site: `vanessa` (school tours: **Questions? Email Vanessa**) and `ava` (pizza: **Pizza questions? Email Ava**). Each has `name`, `email`, `href` (the mailto link, with the subject line the site uses) and `text` (the words of the link). Nothing else about them is shown.
- **A pretend family's records** (the rush) have the same shapes as the family's own, plus `live: true`, `fam`, `ev` and `arrived` (the pretend time in milliseconds). An order also has `num` (the big number, 201 and up) and `familyName`; an order the rush made already confirmed only so a change could be asked for has `base: true`.
- **notice:** `{ id, to: 'family' / 'cathy' / 'kitchen', text, link, at, read }`. **message:** `{ id, thread, from: 'family' / 'cathy', text, at }`; threads are `farm` and `event-<id>`. **review:** `{ id, visitId, stars, text, photoName, thumb, status: 'waiting' / 'approved' / 'declined', at, decided }`. **photo:** `{ id, visitId, name, thumb, status, at, decided }` (`decided` is the time the desk answered). **waitlist:** `{ id, day, party }`. **alerts:** `{ spot, reservations, strawberries, blueberries, pumpkins, trees }` with true or false (see "The Tell me when screen" below).
- **Dates and times** are `YYYY-MM-DD` and `HH:MM` (24 hours), the farm's own clock. `DemoData` writes them as "Saturday, Nov 14" and "2:00 pm".

### The pretend rush (`js/demo-live.js`)

One engine for the desk and the kitchen: `window.DemoLive` (in Node `require('./js/demo-live.js')`, then `create(store, { now, seed })` makes a private engine on any store, which is how the tests run it). **Read the API note at the top of the file first**; it lists every name. The short story:

- **It is off.** Nothing starts by itself; a person presses On on the strip (`DemoLive.strip()` draws the one shared control, `data-tour="live-control"`, with its own style; its On and Off buttons carry `data-dl`, never `data-live`: the site's `js/live.js` hides every `data-live` element that is not an open-now badge). The choice (`on`, `speed`, the answer-by times) lives in the store's object `live`; **Start over** forgets it, and a new ready-made sample (`seedSample()`, which the tour uses) takes the pretend families away and keeps the choices.
- **The pretend families are in the normal lists** with `live: true`, `fam: "Dana's family"` and an `ev` (the number of the event that made them), and ids with an L (`vL3`, `oL3`, `rL3`, `mL3`, `pL3`, `rvL3`; a question is a message in a thread `q-L6`), so the signed-in family's own ids never change. The store **hides them by default** (`list`, `openRequests`, `threadsWaiting`), so the family's screens and the tour never see them; the desk and the kitchen ask with `{ live: true }`. A live record never makes a notice (`reply`, `respond`, `cancel`, `post`, `decide`, `closeDay`, `openDay`, a moved visit), and `decideReview` and `decidePhoto` stamp `decided` on every record.
- **Pretend, from the site's own data.** Pizzas are the menu's (`DemoPizzaData.PIZZAS`, the toppings, the gluten-free crust and the vegan cheese; no price is written on a pretend record), pick-ups are inside the farm's day, a visit change names days the farm is open for that package (`DemoData.openDays`), photos are the farm's own sample pictures, families have first names only and never the signed-in family's name.
- **Deterministic.** Event `k` depends only on the seed and `k`: the same seed and the same clock make the same records. The generator has no real randomness. `tick(now)` is safe to call from any number of pages: an event is made once. A test gives the clock: `create(store, { now: () => ms, seed: 7 })`, then `start()` and `tick(ms)`.
- **The pretend clock** (`time()`) moves only while the rush is on and a page is open (at most 8 seconds between two writes), so a page that was closed for hours does not find every ticket late. **Off is a pause**: no arrivals, and the clock, the timers and the beeps stand still. The pretend farm clock (`clock()`) follows the real one between 10 am and 3 pm and starts at 11:00 am outside those hours, so pick-ups always make sense.
- **It stays small, and it does not stand still.** At most 8 things wait for the kitchen and 10 for the desk. When a side is full and a new thing is due, **the family of the oldest thing of that side gives up** (`giveUpOldest`), if it has waited long enough: the kitchen's things after their answer-by time + 30 seconds (`MISS_GRACE`; so they were late first), the desk's after 2 minutes (`DESK_PATIENCE`), in pretend time. A request is taken back (`status: 'cancelled'`, `missed: true`, `missedAt`, a line in its history: the desk and the kitchen already show a cancelled request), a new order gets `status: 'missed'` (`missed: true`, `missedAt`; the kitchen shows it in its Done lane as "Missed: nobody answered in time" and refuses a late Yes), a question, a photo or a review is removed. Each is counted in `stats().missed`. When both sides are full and nobody has waited long enough yet, the event is not made (`stats().held`, the strip says so) and the next try comes after the next gap: the old engine stopped there for good (Rush after 2 minutes, Busy after 4, Calm after 9). The records of the newest 40 events are kept, the feed keeps 12 events. Two hours of Rush with nobody answering keeps a few dozen records.
- **Speeds:** Calm about one event every 40 seconds, Busy every 18, Rush every 8 (each 0.6 to 1.4 times that). Changing the speed brings the next event at the new pace (never later than one gap of the new speed). About 55 in 100 go to the kitchen: new orders and changes to confirmed orders; the others to the desk: visit changes, questions, photos and reviews.
- **Tour:** a card that shows the rush uses `liveScene()`; every other card of chapter 10 switches the rush off and takes its families away first (`scene()`), so the story reads the same each time.

### The Tell me when screen

`js/demo-view-alerts.js` draws it. From top to bottom:

1. **Where should we send these messages?** Two big switches: **Text message** (it shows the pretend phone number) and **Email** (it shows the account's email). One or both can be on. The last one cannot be turned off: it stays on, and a short note says "Keep at least one on, so we can reach you." Nothing is sent. The choice is only kept in the pretend account.
2. **A spot opens on a full day**, with a day to choose. It puts the family on the waitlist for that day. This is the first choice.
3. **Reservations open**, a plain switch. Its line says only what the site says: reservations with pizza open every Tuesday at 5:00 PM for the upcoming weekend.
4. The farm's seasons, in the site's order: strawberries, blueberries and sunflowers, pumpkins and tomatoes, Christmas trees.
5. **Alerts you got**: the alerts that the desk sent, newest first.

How it is kept:

- The choices come from `DemoData.alertKinds`: for each one a `key`, a `label` (the title), a `note` (the line under it) and a `notice` (what the bell says when the farm sends it). The order of that list is the order on the screen, and the desk reads the same list. To add a choice, add one entry there and a picture and a colour in `js/demo-view-alerts.js`.
- A switch is `alerts[key]`, set with `setAlert(key, bool)`. The key `spot` also has one waitlist entry for the chosen day (`{ id, day, party, active }`). Choosing another day, or switching off, sets the old entry to `active: false`. The desk's "Send the alert" for that day makes the notice "A spot opened on Saturday, Nov 14." (`fireAlert('spot', day)`).
- Where to send is `family.notifyBy`, `{ text: bool, email: bool }`. Both are on until the family changes it. `getNotify()` reads it. `setNotify({ text, email })` changes one or both and answers with the new choice. When the change would turn both off it answers `null` and saves nothing. It tells the page with the same `wa-demo-change` event as every other change. It lives in the family record, so Start over and a new sample put both back on, and a state saved before it existed reads as both on.
- The pretend phone number is `family.phone`. The sample family has `DemoData.SAMPLE_PHONE`. It is a made-up number: never put a real one there. The screen shows the sample number when a family has none, and the account's own email next to **Email**.
- The desk's "People waiting" box lists the seasons and reservations from `DemoData.alertKinds` (one row each) and then a row for each day somebody waits for.
