Farm safety

Wise Acres is a working farm. We take extra precautions to keep it safe, but the farm is not childproof. Irrigation lines, support stakes and farm equipment may be present throughout the fields.

Children must be supervised at all times. Unsupervised children can be injured and may also damage delicate flowers and plants.

Farm & property guidelines

We work hard to grow beautiful flowers and keep the farm photo-ready. In return, we ask all visitors to respect our family home and property.

  • Vehicles are not allowed in the fields, barn or garden areas under any circumstances
  • Please clean up after yourself and remove all trash
  • Any damage to the property may result in additional charges
  • Animals are not permitted on the farm. If you have a registered service dog, please contact us in advance
Additional notes

Because of farming, weather and public visitation, we can’t guarantee that other guests won’t appear in the background of your photos during business hours, or that crop conditions will be ideal.

Please leave the farm when your photography session ends, as the farm closes fully afterward.

Who we are

It started with our kids

The way Wise Acres got its start may be a little unconventional, but the reason behind it is simple: our kids.

Bailey, Morgan and Mac inspired us to grow the healthiest, most delicious berries we possibly can. After leaving our hometown of Chicago and moving our family to beautiful North Carolina, we broke ground on the farm in March of 2013 and began working toward a dream we had been talking about for years.

Before farming, we worked in finance and business. What we really wanted was a place where our kids could enjoy the same opportunities we had growing up, with the space, fresh air and outdoor life we all love. After a long search, we found the perfect piece of land in Indian Trail.

When we first stepped onto the property, we quickly realized we had a lot of work ahead of us. So we rolled up our sleeves and got started. We have big plans for the future of the farm, and we’re grateful for the amazing customers who have helped Wise Acres grow along the way.

Our berries

Our family lives on the farm, and we eat the berries we grow, so we care deeply about how they’re produced. We grow our strawberries without the poisons commonly used in conventional strawberry farming. Many of those chemicals don’t just stay in the field. They can end up in the fruit, the soil and the water we share with our neighbors.

Strawberries regularly rank at the top of the Environmental Working Group’s “Dirty Dozen” list for pesticide contamination. That’s one more reason we grow berries in a way that’s healthier for our family, our customers and our community.

Organic

Wise Acres is a USDA Certified Organic farm. Our practices include crop rotation, cover crops, detailed record keeping and regular third-party inspections to make sure we meet all current organic standards.

These practices help us care for our soil, protect the environment and grow berries you can feel good about eating. We are committed to providing the healthiest berries possible.

Local

Most produce in supermarkets is harvested 5–7 days before it reaches the shelves. During that time, natural sugars begin turning to starch, and fruit gradually loses flavor and nutrients. Many grocery-store varieties are chosen because they ship well, not because they taste best.

On average, food travels about 1,500 miles before reaching the supermarket. When you buy local, you get produce that was picked fresh and is ready to eat, and you support lower energy use and less packaging.

Care & handling

We do our very best to grow the sweetest, most flavorful berries possible. Once you leave the farm, a few simple steps keep them at their best.

  1. Refrigerate your berries as soon as you get home.
  2. Don’t wash them until you’re ready to serve.
  3. Rinse gently under cool water with the green caps still attached.
  4. Let them warm to room temperature before serving for the best flavor.

      When are you open?

      Our seasons vary each year with weather and crop conditions.

      • Spring, strawberries: usually mid-April through early June
      • Summer, blueberries & sunflowers: usually mid- to late June through early July
      • Fall, pumpkins: mid-September through early November
      • Fall, tomatoes & basil (new this year): every weekend, late September through October
      • Winter, NC Christmas trees: the Friday after Thanksgiving to early December, at The GreenHouse
      • U-cut flowers: April–July and September–first frost

      Exact opening dates depend on Mother Nature. We share updates as crops begin to ripen, so join our email list.

      Can I visit the farm without a reservation?

      No. We are open by reservation only. Please don’t come to the farm without one.

      Wise Acres is a small family farm in a neighborhood with limited parking. When we’re not open to the public, we’re working in the fields. Reservations let us manage parking, protect the crops and give guests, neighbors and farm families a safe, enjoyable experience.

      We’re the only USDA-certified organic strawberry farm in the area, and we grow far fewer berries than conventional farms. Organic practices mean we plant only about one-third of our fields at a time, so supply is limited. In the past, over-picking damaged crops and cost the farm revenue. Reservations help us protect the farm while giving visitors the best experience. Thank you for your patience and support.

      Want to visit without a reservation? Stop by our adjacent property, The GreenHouse at 5503. No reservation is required, and hours vary with the weather.

      When do reservations open?

      Fall: reservations without pizza open a month or more at a time, and reservations with pizza open every Tuesday at 5:00 PM for the upcoming weekend. See the current fall schedule.

      Often, when reservations first open, there’s so much traffic that the site jams and shows an error message. That does not mean reservations are full. Please check back after the initial rush.

      Spring: reservations open once we begin seeing fruit ripen, usually a few weeks before the season starts. Strawberry season is our busiest time and reservations fill quickly. Production follows a bell curve, with peak picking typically in late April through early May. Early in the season we release only a small number of reservations, and we usually open more dates weekly as we watch field conditions and weather.

      Other seasons generally allow more visitors, but availability is still affected by parking limits, county permits, staffing and weather.

      How can I enjoy Wise Pie pizza?

      The county limits the number of cooking-permit days and hours we’re allowed each season, so we don’t post dates until our permits are approved.

      At the farm until 4:00 PM: only with a farm reservation.
      At The GreenHouse, Friday–Sunday, 4:00–8:00 PM: no reservation required, but no farm access. This gives neighbors, and anyone who only wants one pizza, an easy option.

      More details in the Wise Pie section.

      Do you have u-pick tomatoes and basil?

      Yes, and it’s new this year! Certified organic cherry tomatoes, slicers and basil are available to u-pick every weekend from late September through October. Tomatoes are $4.50 per pound and basil is $1 per stem.

      See every variety in the tomato & basil guide.

      Where can I get a Christmas tree?

      Christmas trees are at The GreenHouse (5503 Poplin Rd). Tree season starts the Friday after Thanksgiving and runs to early December. No reservation is needed at The GreenHouse.

      Are dogs allowed?

      Unfortunately, no. We love dogs, but because of liability and food-safety regulations, pets must stay home. Service animals are welcome.

      What forms of payment do you accept?

      Cash is preferred. We also accept all major credit cards, Apple Pay and Google Pay.

      What is your rain policy?

      We’re farmers, so a little rain doesn’t close the farm.

      • You can change your reservation at no charge until 11:59 PM the night before your visit
      • If you cancel by then, we refund your reservation minus the 3% credit card processing fee
      • If severe weather forces us to close, we’ll email you and give a full refund
      How do I change or cancel my reservation?

      Open your confirmation or reminder email and click the “View Booking” button at the bottom. It takes you straight to your reservation page, where you can make changes.

      Reservations are transferable. The person attending can simply check in under the reservation name.

      What are your bathroom facilities?

      We currently offer porta-johns, including a handicapped-accessible unit, and portable foot-pump hand-washing stations throughout the farm.

      Our farm uses well water and a septic system, which aren’t approved for commercial use. For over a decade we’ve requested city water and sewer, and those requests have been denied. Until that changes, portable facilities are our only option. Thank you for your understanding.
